
# PREPARING FOR A POWER DISTRIBUTION ENGINEER CAREER

**By Evan Liang**

Preparing for a career in power distribution engineering requires more than completing electrical engineering coursework. This report outlines the best practices for developing the practical experience and technical skills needed to pursue an entry-level position in power distribution engineering. Understanding these practices will help you prepare for the responsibilities required to succeed in the field.

---

## GAINING PRACTICAL EXPERIENCE

Gaining industry experience involves finding engineering internships and learning from experienced professionals in the industry.

### Finding Internships

Engineering internships provide practical experience in the field that will demonstrate the responsibilities of electrical engineers. These opportunities allow you to apply coursework knowledge to engineering work outside the classroom.

Begin searching for engineering internships during your third year or fourth year of college, when you have developed a foundation in electrical engineering. Attend university career fairs or use LinkedIn to connect with engineering professionals and learn about potential internship opportunities.

### Learning from Experienced Engineers

Working alongside experienced engineers gives you hands-on experience with engineering projects and problems that may not be covered in textbooks or classroom lessons.

Observe how experienced engineers solve practical problems, ask questions to learn more about the job duties, and assist with assigned engineering tasks.

---

## BUILDING TECHNICAL SKILLS

This section explains how to develop the technical knowledge and skills needed through understanding engineering software, electrical codes, safety standards, and how to apply coursework knowledge to hands-on projects.

### Engineering Software

AutoCAD is a commonly used software in engineering to create and share technical drawings. Becoming familiar with AutoCAD will help you interpret engineering designs and communicate more effectively with other engineers. These skills also prepare you to work with engineering drawings before entering the role [3].

Look for opportunities to develop AutoCAD skills through university coursework or introductory training courses. Apply these skills in engineering projects to gain technical experience using the software.

### Electrical Codes and Safety Standards

Understanding the National Electrical Code (NEC) and National Electrical Safety Code (NESC) helps you recognize the required safety standards when designing and installing electrical systems. These regulations provide guidelines that allow engineers to make informed decisions and ensure the safety and reliability of power systems [1], [2].

Learn about the NEC and NESC through electrical engineering coursework, training programs or independent study using online resources.

### Hands-On Engineering Projects

Hands-on engineering projects allow you to apply coursework knowledge to real-world electrical problems. These will help you build technical skills by designing systems, testing their function, identifying potential problems, and finding solutions.

Explore opportunities to participate in university engineering projects or engineering clubs. Engage in activities that are focused on designing, testing, and troubleshooting electrical systems to strengthen your technical knowledge before graduation.

---

## DEVELOPING JOB APPLICATIONS & NETWORKING

Applying for the position involves preparing a professional technical resume and establishing connections with professionals in the power distribution industry.

### Building a Technical Resume

A technical resume provides employers with an outline of your qualifications, allowing them to evaluate and determine whether your skills match their engineering positions. Highlighting relevant technical skills, coursework, and experience can demonstrate how your credentials align with the job’s requirements.

Prepare a resume that emphasizes your electrical engineering knowledge, technical skills, and internship experience. Research the roles you are applying for and practice responding to potential interview questions.

### Networking with Professionals

Building professional connections helps you learn about career opportunities and understand what employers expect from entry-level engineers.

Attend university career fairs and connect with engineers and recruiters through LinkedIn. Ask about their work experience, engineering responsibilities, and qualifications employers look for in new graduates.
